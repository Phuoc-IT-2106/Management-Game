# EXPANSION PRINCIPLES

The project has a broad long-term vision, but broad vision must not create broad early implementation.

## Principle 1 — Shared Core First
Build reusable company-level concepts before adding many industries.

Examples:
- finance
- people
- contracts
- organization
- reputation
- audience
- time
- risk

## Principle 2 — Domain Modules
A sports or business domain should add only the rules specific to that domain.

Example:

Company Core
+ Esports Module
= Esports Division

Company Core
+ Media Module
= Media Division

## Principle 3 — Expansion Must Create Decisions
A new division is valuable only if it creates new strategic decisions.

Bad expansion:
"Buy media company → +10% money."

Better expansion:
A media division increases audience reach but requires staff, capital, scheduling, brand management, and may create conflicts with sponsors or competitive priorities.

## Principle 4 — Synergy Is Not Free
Cross-division synergies should require conditions or investment.

Examples:
- esports popularity improves advertising inventory
- media improves sponsor reach
- events improve brand awareness
- sports facilities create infrastructure costs
- shared staff lowers cost but creates capacity bottlenecks

## Principle 5 — Growth Creates Complexity
Expansion should increase:
- coordination requirements
- management overhead
- financial exposure
- reputation risk
- staffing needs

Large companies should not simply be small companies with larger numbers.

## Principle 6 — Delegation
As the company grows, the player should be able to delegate operational work while retaining strategic control.

Delegation quality may depend on:
- staff skill
- authority
- trust
- information
- incentives
- organizational structure

## Principle 7 — Validate Before Expanding
Do not add a new industry until the current company simulation already produces meaningful decisions.

## Principle 8 — Avoid Universal Simulation
Do not simulate every real-world business process.

Simulate what creates:
- meaningful decisions
- strategic consequences
- useful uncertainty
- player feedback
