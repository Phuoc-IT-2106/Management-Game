# CORE LOOP

Status: Phase 1 — Product Foundation proposal

## 1. Core Management Loop

**PROPOSAL**

> Observe → Prioritize → Decide → Commit → Delegate / Intervene → Advance Time → Resolve Competition & Business Consequences → Review → Adapt

This loop extends the Phase 0 reasoning rhythm without defining technical simulation architecture.

## 2. Loop Stages

### 1. Observe

The player receives a decision-oriented view of:

- company health;
- cash / financial pressure;
- roster and staff condition;
- upcoming competition;
- sponsor/commercial status;
- reputation / audience signals;
- rival activity;
- risks, opportunities, and warnings;
- staff recommendations.

**Design requirement:** information should answer "what needs executive attention?" rather than expose all available data.

### 2. Prioritize

The player identifies which problems or opportunities deserve limited resources and attention.

Examples:

- competitive weakness;
- contract expiry;
- sponsor opportunity;
- staff issue;
- financial pressure;
- rebuilding opportunity.

**PROPOSAL**

Not every visible problem should be solvable simultaneously.

### 3. Decide

The player makes executive or management choices.

Examples:

- approve a signing;
- change a budget;
- accept/reject a sponsor;
- replace a coach;
- set preparation priority;
- preserve cash;
- take strategic financial risk;
- choose a restructuring response.

### 4. Commit

Decisions consume or expose resources such as:

- money;
- time;
- roster slots;
- staff capacity;
- reputation;
- information;
- flexibility;
- risk tolerance.

**PROPOSAL**

Commitment should often create delayed consequences so the player cannot judge every decision instantly.

### 5. Delegate / Intervene

The player decides how much operational authority to give staff.

Possible actions:

- accept a recommendation;
- modify it;
- define constraints;
- override a decision;
- fully delegate.

This stage is important because the product fantasy is executive leadership rather than total manual control.

### 6. Advance Time

The simulation moves forward through matches, contract developments, financial obligations, market activity, rival actions, and organizational changes.

**OPEN QUESTION**

The exact time unit and advancement model belong to Phase 2.

### 7. Resolve Interconnected Consequences

Outcomes should propagate across systems.

Example:

Competitive result
→ reputation / audience
→ sponsor or commercial value
→ cash and opportunity
→ roster / staff investment capacity
→ future competitive capability.

Negative feedback and diminishing returns are required so this does not become automatic exponential growth.

### 8. Review

The player receives understandable feedback:

- what changed;
- why it changed;
- which prior decisions contributed;
- what new risks or opportunities appeared;
- where uncertainty remains.

### 9. Adapt

The player updates priorities and enters the next cycle.

The repeated activity is reasoning under changing constraints, not executing an identical checklist.

## 3. Nested Loop Structure

**PROPOSAL**

### Short loop — Situation / Match Window

Observe immediate state
→ prepare / respond
→ delegate or intervene
→ resolve
→ review.

### Medium loop — Competition / Contract Cycle

Evaluate roster, staff, finances, sponsors
→ commit resources
→ compete
→ adjust strategy.

### Long loop — Company Evolution

Set strategic direction
→ invest
→ accept risk
→ survive growth and setbacks
→ restructure or expand
→ reassess identity and capability.

These loops should share state rather than operate as separate mini-games.

## 4. Anti-Repetition Requirement

**DECISION**

Variation should come from changing state and system interactions, not primarily from random event text.

**PROPOSAL**

A loop iteration should become materially different when one or more of these change:

- available cash;
- contract timing;
- talent availability;
- opponent strength;
- coach quality;
- sponsor priorities;
- reputation;
- company expectations;
- rival behavior;
- information quality;
- organizational capacity;
- risk exposure.

## 5. Failure / Recovery Loop

**DECISION**

Structural failure should usually produce recovery gameplay before game over.

**PROPOSAL**

Financial distress loop:

Warning
→ liquidity pressure
→ emergency prioritization
→ restructuring choices
→ short-term sacrifice
→ recovery attempt
→ stabilization or terminal failure.

The player should understand which commitments created the crisis and which sacrifices are required to escape it.

## 6. First Vertical-Slice Loop

**PROPOSAL**

For the first vertical slice, the smallest complete product loop should be:

1. Review company, roster, staff, sponsor, and upcoming competition state.
2. Choose spending and preparation priorities.
3. Make one or more meaningful roster/staff/contract/commercial decisions when available.
4. Delegate or manually influence high-level competitive preparation.
5. Advance to competition and business resolution.
6. Receive results and cross-system consequences.
7. React to changed finances, reputation, opportunities, and risks.
8. Repeat across enough cycles to force adaptation.

## 7. Vertical-Slice Acceptance Signal

**PROPOSAL**

The loop is product-valid when:

- multiple reasonable strategies can survive;
- the same response is not optimal every cycle;
- competitive and business decisions visibly interact;
- delegation reduces burden but preserves accountability;
- financial pressure creates meaningful trade-offs;
- rivals create external pressure;
- a setback can generate recovery decisions;
- the player can explain why the company improved or deteriorated.

## 8. Open Questions for Phase 2

**OPEN QUESTION**

- What is the time model?
- What are the authoritative resource pools?
- What events trigger decisions?
- How are rival organizations updated?
- How are recommendations generated conceptually?
- Which consequences are immediate versus delayed?
- How is match resolution represented at medium depth?
