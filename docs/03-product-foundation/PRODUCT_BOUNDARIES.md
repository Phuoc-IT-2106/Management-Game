# PRODUCT BOUNDARIES

Status: Phase 1 — Product Foundation proposal

## 1. Core Product Promise

**PROPOSAL**

> Lead a professional esports organization as an executive, make consequential decisions across competition and business, and turn limited resources, people, reputation, and competitive results into a resilient and increasingly valuable company inside a changing ecosystem of rival organizations.

The promise depends on four properties:

1. Decisions have meaningful opportunity cost.
2. Competitive and business systems affect one another.
3. The world changes, so yesterday's good strategy may stop being optimal.
4. Growth increases both capability and organizational difficulty.

## 2. What the Game Is

### Company-first management simulation

**DECISION**

The strategic center is the company, beginning with esports as the validation domain.

### Executive + management game

**DECISION**

The player primarily makes high-impact executive and management decisions.

### Competitive-business interaction

**DECISION**

Competitive performance matters because it creates or destroys business leverage.

### Medium-depth simulation with selective depth

**DECISION**

Simulation is medium depth by default. Deeper modeling is justified only where it creates meaningful decisions, emergent behavior, useful uncertainty, or long-term consequences.

### Evolving competitive ecosystem

**DECISION**

Rival organizations should evolve and compete for relevant resources and opportunities rather than remain static background entities.

### Failure with recovery gameplay

**DECISION**

Structural failure is real. Financial distress, debt pressure, forced downsizing, restructuring, or asset sales should usually create a recovery window before terminal game over.

## 3. What the Game Is Not

**DECISION**

The early product is not:

- a full multi-industry corporate simulator;
- a global macroeconomic simulation;
- a detailed accounting package;
- a complete esports tactical simulator;
- a daily-task micromanagement game;
- a simulation of every employee's life;
- a collection of disconnected subsystem mini-games;
- a content-heavy event game whose variety depends mainly on scripted events;
- a game where winning matches is the sole measure of success;
- a game where growth automatically means success.

## 4. Simulation Details That Should Remain Abstract

**PROPOSAL**

Unless later testing proves strategic value, abstract or simplify:

- payroll/accounting line-item administration;
- taxes and legal compliance detail;
- travel logistics;
- routine scheduling;
- low-level HR administration;
- minute-by-minute training;
- full physical facility simulation;
- detailed social media posting;
- complete fan-by-fan behavior;
- complete match execution;
- every staff member's daily task queue;
- all real-world esports governance details;
- full macroeconomic modeling.

Abstract systems may still generate costs, risks, recommendations, or constraints without exposing operational detail.

## 5. First Vertical-Slice Product Boundary

**PROPOSAL**

The narrow first vertical slice should prove the company-management fantasy using:

### Included

- 1 player-controlled company;
- 1 esports discipline;
- 1 primary competitive team;
- a small roster;
- a small but meaningful staff structure;
- several evolving rival organizations in the same competitive ecosystem;
- a limited competition cycle;
- roster and key staff decisions;
- contracts at decision-relevant depth;
- basic finance and cash pressure;
- one or a small number of sponsor relationships;
- reputation / audience / commercial-value linkage;
- competitive preparation at high-leverage decision depth;
- delegation to a coach/staff role;
- time progression;
- at least one credible financial distress / restructuring path;
- observable consequences connecting competitive performance and company state.

### Explicitly excluded from the first vertical slice

- additional industries;
- multiple esports disciplines;
- multiple player-controlled teams;
- detailed facilities;
- advanced media business;
- merchandise operations as a deep subsystem;
- detailed debt markets;
- acquisitions and corporate M&A;
- full tactical match simulation;
- large global economy;
- complete organization-chart simulation;
- deep employee relationship networks;
- large authored event libraries.

## 6. Vertical-Slice Validation Question

**PROPOSAL**

The slice succeeds if repeated play creates questions such as:

- Should I spend scarce cash on immediate competitive strength or preserve runway?
- Is a star player worth the contract risk and loss of future flexibility?
- Should I trust the coach's plan or intervene?
- Should I accept a sponsor whose money helps now but constrains brand or competitive choices?
- Can I afford to sacrifice short-term results to rebuild?
- Which problem deserves executive attention now?
- When financial pressure appears, what can I cut without damaging the company's future?

If these decisions remain meaningful after repetition, the foundation is working.

## 7. Scope Guard

**DECISION**

Broad long-term vision must not produce broad early implementation.

**OPEN QUESTION**

The exact size of the rival ecosystem, competition calendar, and sponsor market remains to be specified during Core Loop & Simulation.
