# PLAYER ROLE

Status: Phase 1 — Product Foundation proposal built from accepted Phase 0 directions

## 1. Primary Player Fantasy

**DECISION**

The player primarily acts as the CEO / executive leader of a professional esports organization.

The fantasy is not "control every task inside an esports team." It is:

> Build, steer, grow, and protect an esports company by making high-impact executive and management decisions, using competitive performance as a source of business leverage while adapting to an evolving competitive and commercial ecosystem.

The player is accountable for the overall direction of the company, not only for match results.

Competitive success matters because it can strengthen reputation, fandom, commercial value, talent attraction, bargaining power, and future organizational capability. It is therefore both a sporting objective and a strategic company asset.

## 2. Authority Model

### Executive Level

**DECISION**

The executive layer is the player's primary authority domain.

The player owns decisions such as:

- company priorities and strategic direction;
- capital allocation and high-level budgets;
- major investments and financial risk;
- leadership appointments;
- organizational structure;
- major partnerships and commercial commitments;
- whether to prioritize growth, stability, competitive strength, or restructuring;
- when to accept, avoid, or reduce strategic risk.

The player should receive enough information to understand material consequences without being required to perform accounting or administrative bookkeeping.

### Management Level

**DECISION**

The player remains directly involved in management decisions that materially affect company outcomes.

Examples:

- roster strategy;
- signing, retaining, selling, or releasing key players;
- key staff hiring;
- important contracts;
- sponsor selection and negotiation direction;
- scouting priorities;
- competitive preparation priorities;
- department-level resource allocation;
- choosing whether to intervene in a problem or delegate it.

Management decisions should connect upward to company strategy rather than behave as isolated subsystem chores.

### Optional / Manual Competitive Control

**DECISION**

Competitive management may support a deeper manual mode for players who want it.

Potential player involvement includes:

- opponent analysis;
- preparation focus;
- strategic approach;
- roles or lineups;
- drafting where discipline-relevant;
- tactical instructions;
- risk posture;
- selected situational or mid-match decisions.

Manual competitive control is optional depth, not the mandatory center of the product.

**PROPOSAL**

For the first vertical slice, manual competitive control should expose only a small number of high-leverage decisions rather than a complete tactical simulator.

### Delegated / Abstracted Operational Control

**DECISION**

Low-value operational work should normally be abstracted, simplified, or delegated.

Examples:

- repetitive daily schedules;
- routine training administration;
- low-impact staff tasks;
- detailed logistics;
- minor tactical adjustments;
- repetitive administrative workflows.

Coaches and staff may operate within assigned authority, produce recommendations, and make operational decisions when delegated.

The player's role is to:

- define priorities;
- select capable people;
- set constraints and authority;
- review outcomes;
- intervene when strategically justified.

## 3. Responsibility Boundary

**DECISION**

The player is responsible for outcomes that reasonably belong to executive and management leadership, including:

- company viability;
- strategic positioning;
- resource allocation;
- major people decisions;
- competitive capability;
- commercial relationships;
- organizational resilience;
- response to structural risk.

The player is not expected to personally perform every activity that contributes to those outcomes.

## 4. Information Boundary

**PROPOSAL**

The player should receive decision-relevant information at the level needed to exercise executive judgment:

- current company condition;
- expected trade-offs;
- warnings and constraints;
- forecasts or staff recommendations;
- uncertainty where information quality is imperfect;
- consequences of prior decisions.

The game should avoid exposing data merely because the simulation contains it.

## 5. Delegation Principle

**DECISION**

Delegation becomes increasingly important as organizational complexity grows.

**PROPOSAL**

Delegation should itself become a management decision through:

- who receives authority;
- how broad that authority is;
- how much the player trusts recommendations;
- when the player overrides staff;
- what information staff can access;
- how staff quality changes decision quality.

Delegation should reduce operational burden without removing strategic accountability.

## 6. Player Identity Summary

**DECISION**

The player is best represented as an executive leader with selective hands-on management authority.

The product should not frame the player primarily as:

- a coach;
- a tactical match operator;
- an accountant;
- a daily scheduler;
- an omnipotent controller of every employee action.

## 7. Open Questions

**OPEN QUESTION**

- Is the player explicitly the founder/owner-CEO, a hired CEO, or an abstract executive identity?
- Can the player later be removed from leadership, or is company failure the only terminal leadership consequence?
- How visible should staff recommendations and confidence levels be?
- Which competitive decisions are mandatory in the first vertical slice versus optional manual depth?
