# CORE DECISIONS

Status: Phase 1 — Product Foundation proposal

## 1. Decision Design Rule

**PROPOSAL**

A recurring player decision belongs in the core product only when it has:

- a meaningful objective;
- relevant but imperfect information;
- at least two reasonable options;
- a real opportunity cost or risk;
- consequences across one or more systems;
- a possibility that changing conditions alter the preferred choice.

Routine confirmation clicks and obvious dominant choices should be automated, delegated, merged, or removed.

## 2. Short-Term Decisions

Typical horizon: immediate situation to the next match / short operating window.

### Competitive preparation

**DECISION**

The player may choose preparation priorities and optionally intervene in competitive strategy.

Trade-offs may include:

- specific opponent preparation vs general development;
- short-term form vs longer-term improvement;
- safe strategy vs higher-risk upside;
- trusting staff vs overriding recommendations.

### Immediate roster availability

**PROPOSAL**

React to injury-equivalent availability, form, conflict, fatigue, discipline-specific suitability, or short-term substitution needs at an abstracted level.

### Cash-pressure response

**DECISION**

When obligations become urgent, the player may need to preserve liquidity, delay discretionary spending, sell contracts/assets, or accept unfavorable financing/restructuring.

### Sponsor / stakeholder response

**PROPOSAL**

Respond to time-sensitive sponsor opportunities, demands, conflicts, or risks when they materially affect money, reputation, or flexibility.

## 3. Medium-Term Decisions

Typical horizon: several competitive events to a season / contract cycle.

### Roster strategy

**DECISION**

Choose whether to:

- retain current core;
- replace weak links;
- invest in a star;
- develop cheaper talent;
- sell valuable talent;
- rebuild.

This decision should affect performance, payroll, flexibility, reputation, and future market options.

### Key staffing

**DECISION**

Hire, retain, replace, or empower coaches and important staff.

Staff should matter because they change:

- operational quality;
- information quality;
- recommendations;
- delegation capacity;
- development;
- organizational stability.

### Contract commitment

**DECISION**

Choose contract length, financial commitment, retention strategy, and risk exposure at meaningful but non-bookkeeping depth.

### Sponsor portfolio

**DECISION**

Evaluate sponsor value beyond immediate cash.

Potential considerations:

- guaranteed income;
- performance expectations;
- reputation fit;
- audience fit;
- exclusivity;
- strategic restrictions;
- relationship risk.

### Department / capability investment

**DECISION**

Allocate limited resources among capabilities such as:

- scouting;
- analytics;
- coaching;
- player development;
- commercial function;
- organizational support.

The first slice should include only a limited subset.

### Competitive ambition

**PROPOSAL**

Set an operating posture such as contend now, balanced development, rebuild, or stabilize.

This is not necessarily a literal UI mode; it can be represented through linked decisions.

## 4. Long-Term Decisions

Typical horizon: multiple seasons / major company transitions.

### Company growth strategy

**DECISION**

Balance:

- competitive strength;
- financial stability;
- reputation;
- audience;
- organizational capacity;
- strategic flexibility.

### Risk posture

**DECISION**

Use or avoid leverage, expensive commitments, aggressive growth, and other strategies that can accelerate progress but expose the company to structural failure.

### Organizational design and delegation

**DECISION**

As complexity increases, decide which functions remain under direct control and which are entrusted to staff.

### Strategic identity

**PROPOSAL**

The company may gradually develop a recognizable strategic identity through repeated choices, such as:

- talent-development focused;
- star-driven;
- financially conservative;
- aggressively growth-oriented;
- sponsor/commercial focused;
- competition-first within sustainable limits.

These should emerge from decisions rather than require a rigid class selection.

### Expansion beyond the initial esports domain

**DECISION — FUTURE, NOT FIRST SLICE**

The long-term product may expand into additional industries, but this belongs after the core company-management model is validated.

## 5. Cross-Horizon Tension

**PROPOSAL**

The strongest recurring decisions should connect all three horizons.

Example:

Signing an expensive star may:

- improve next-match potential;
- change season-level salary structure and team dynamics;
- increase sponsor appeal and reputation;
- reduce cash runway;
- weaken future roster flexibility;
- increase expectations;
- become dangerous if competitive results fail.

The decision is interesting because no single outcome determines whether it was correct.

## 6. Decisions to Avoid as Core Gameplay

**PROPOSAL**

Do not make the player repeatedly decide:

- routine training schedules with obvious answers;
- individual travel arrangements;
- repetitive invoice/payment administration;
- low-impact staff scheduling;
- trivial contract paperwork;
- obvious roster confirmations;
- cosmetic choices with no strategic consequence unless presented explicitly as customization.

## 7. Open Questions

**OPEN QUESTION**

- Which 3–5 resource pools are essential to the first prototype beyond money?
- How should audience, reputation, and commercial value differ mechanically?
- Which staff roles exist in the first vertical slice?
- What minimum competitive decisions are needed for manual mode?
- What information is hidden, estimated, or forecast?
