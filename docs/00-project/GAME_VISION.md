# GAME VISION

## Product Thesis
A strategy management simulation in which the player grows a focused esports organization into a broader company by managing people, capital, brands, competitive divisions, commercial opportunities, and organizational risk in a changing market.

## Player Experience
The game should make the player think:

- Where should I invest?
- Which opportunity is worth the risk?
- Which division deserves resources?
- Should I grow now or preserve cash?
- Which people are critical?
- Is this sponsor or partnership strategically useful?
- Should I enter a new market?
- How much complexity can the company sustain?
- Which problems are temporary and which require structural change?

## Design Pillars

### 1. Strategic Trade-offs
Important decisions should have meaningful opportunity cost.

### 2. Interconnected Systems
Competitive performance, people, money, reputation, audience, sponsors, and expansion should influence one another.

### 3. Changing Environment
Markets, competitors, talent, regulations, trends, and opportunities may evolve over time.

### 4. Organizational Growth
Growth should create both new capabilities and new management problems.

### 5. Emergent Stories
Stories should often arise from simulation:
- a star player leaves
- a sponsor becomes unhappy
- one division subsidizes another
- a risky expansion succeeds
- internal leadership conflict appears
- a weak department becomes unexpectedly valuable

### 6. Information-Based Decisions
The player should not always have perfect information. Better scouting, analytics, staff, and market knowledge should improve decisions.

## Long-Term Company Fantasy
Possible future evolution:

Esports Organization
→ Multi-Team Esports Company
→ Sports & Entertainment Group
→ Media / Advertising / Events
→ Multi-division Strategic Company

This is a direction, not a mandatory linear progression.

## Anti-Repetition Principle
Variation should emerge from changing state, not simply from random text events.

Use:
- market shifts
- talent development
- competitive meta
- contracts
- staff turnover
- financial pressure
- strategic rivals
- sponsor priorities
- reputation
- audience trends
- organizational capacity
- expansion decisions
- external shocks

Randomness should create decisions, not replace them.
