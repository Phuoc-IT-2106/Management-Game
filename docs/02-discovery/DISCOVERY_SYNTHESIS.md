# 1. Current Product Interpretation

The project should not be treated primarily as an "esports team manager."

The stronger long-term identity is:

> A strategic company management simulation that begins with esports as its first proving ground.

The first playable product should remain intentionally narrow even though the long-term product vision is broad.

There are three nested layers implied by the current concept:

## Layer 1 — Competitive Organization Management
Examples:
- roster
- staff
- preparation
- competition
- contracts

## Layer 2 — Company Management
Examples:
- finance
- sponsorship
- reputation
- organizational capacity
- resource allocation

## Layer 3 — Strategic Company Building
Examples:
- new divisions
- new markets
- cross-division synergies
- delegation
- structural risk
- executive strategy

For the first playable product:

- Layer 1 should generate pressure and consequences.
- Layer 2 should be the main decision space.
- Most of Layer 3 should remain future-facing until the management model is proven.

---

# 2. Product Thesis v0.1

PROPOSAL — not yet locked.

> A PC strategy-management simulation where the player leads a growing professional esports organization, allocating limited resources and organizational capacity while adapting to changing people, markets, competition, opportunities, and risks.

The central challenge is not simply winning matches.

The deeper challenge is building an organization capable of making good strategic decisions as both the external environment and internal complexity evolve.

Proposed basic decision rhythm:

Observe
→ Decide
→ Commit Resources
→ Advance Time
→ Experience Interconnected Consequences
→ Adapt

The player should repeat the reasoning process, not repeat the exact same decision.

# 3. Strongest Conceptual Advantages

## Interconnected Systems
Competitive results, reputation, sponsors, money, staff, talent, information, and organizational capacity can influence one another.

## Esports as Validation Domain
Esports provides enough complexity to test:
- people
- contracts
- performance
- competition
- sponsorship
- reputation
- uncertainty

without requiring a complete global business simulation.

## Systemic Anti-Repetition
Variation should come from changing constraints, opportunities, information, timing, and system relationships.

## Growth as Gameplay
Growth should increase both capability and organizational difficulty.

A stronger company should not simply be the same company with larger numbers.

## Emergent Situations
Persistent simulation state should be capable of producing:
- personnel crises
- sponsor conflicts
- leadership tensions
- budget pressure
- strategic opportunities
- unexpected cross-system consequences

without requiring every situation to be manually scripted.

---

# 4. Current Strategic Principle

Do not simulate something merely because it exists in reality.

Simulate it when it creates:
- a meaningful decision,
- a meaningful consequence,
- useful uncertainty,
- or important feedback for the player.

This principle should be used later to control simulation scope.
