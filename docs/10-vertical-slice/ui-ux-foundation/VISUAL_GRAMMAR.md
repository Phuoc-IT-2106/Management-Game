# Visual grammar

Status: PROPOSAL. Representation families are semantic placeholders, not final
icons. Every representation has a readable label and stable target ID.

| Class | Semantic meaning | Representation family | State modifiers | Selection behavior | Overlay/status rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Player Company | Highest owned context | Identity mark + name + scope container | Active campaign, identity unavailable | Open company context; retain current route in history | Safe brand accent only; risk appears separately |
| Division | Owned business scope | Branch/container symbol + domain label | Active, unavailable, future absent | Open scoped workspace | Aggregate only material child situations; explain count |

## Relationship grammar

Containment means ownership/scope; an arrow labelled “funds,” “constrains” or
“informs” means a specific relationship, not ownership. Reporting authority and
commercial agreements use distinct labels. A dotted/annotated estimated relation
cannot look like a known signed commitment. Unknown connections are not invented.

Financial arrows show source → Cash → use, with due time and units. Opportunity
links are not cash-flow arrows. A consequence arrow represents an owner-applied
causal relationship, not permission for UI or Competition to write Finance.

Selection outline, keyboard focus, semantic warning and company brand occupy
separate channels. Prioritize readable labels and one primary status with details
on demand over a pile of tiny badges. Status counts are scoped and deduplicated
by cause; a company warning may summarize the same obligation shown locally.

An absent emblem/portrait has a stable text/symbol fallback. Icon meaning must be
learnable through accompanying labels, focus descriptions and consistent use.
Do not encode entity type only by color. Placeholder/vector assets must support
every core interaction before art is commissioned.
