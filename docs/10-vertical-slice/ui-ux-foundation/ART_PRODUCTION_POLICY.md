# Art production policy

Status: PROPOSAL, under the Director's one-developer/system-first constraint.

Quality should come primarily from information architecture, typography,
composition, hierarchy, consistent reusable components and clear consequences.
Prefer vector icons, symbols, configurable company marks, diagrams, documents,
charts and a small reusable background set. Motion explains focus/context/change
and has a reduced-motion alternative.

| Character tier | Representation | Dependency rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 0 | Name, role and symbolic identity | Required baseline for every workflow |
| Tier 1 | Initials, silhouette or simple reusable avatar | Optional recognition aid; same command/inspection path |
| Tier 2 | Curated portrait for selected important characters | Optional later enhancement with separate approval and maintenance cost |

No workflow depends on Tier 2. Absence of portraits must not conceal person
identity, authority or uncertainty. AI-generated portraits are not a production
dependency. Do not generate them or final UI images in this task.

No mandatory photorealistic headquarters, cinematic character scenes, bespoke
3D environment, illustration per screen or large portrait library. A company map
can use labelled vectors; a negotiation can use readable terms. Logos are optional
player identity references with neutral fallback, never a canonical player logo.

Before approving bespoke art, state its decision/recognition value, reusable
contexts, accessibility fallback, creation and maintenance cost, asset provenance
and responsible owner. If a text/symbol version cannot support the decision,
repair the UX first. Concepts may guide mood after approval; they never specify
component contracts, gameplay mechanics or final pixels by implication.

The later UI Kit owns a small icon/typography system and placeholder assets.
Final font licensing, aesthetic palette and commissioned content remain Director
review matters. No new asset pipeline is authorized here.
