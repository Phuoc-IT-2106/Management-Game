# CONSEQUENCE MAP

Status: Phase 2 — Core Loop & Simulation specialist proposal

## 1. Principle

**DECISION — inherited**

The game must produce interconnected consequences. Competitive performance is a major source of business leverage, but winning is not the only measure of success.

**PROPOSAL**

Consequences should normally form **chains with friction, delay, and counter-pressure**, not direct permanent bonuses.

## 2. Primary Consequence Chains

### Chain A — Competitive success to business leverage

**PROPOSAL**

Competition result
→ performance credibility
→ reputation change
→ audience attention / retention
→ sponsor attractiveness / commercial value
→ cash opportunity
→ roster/staff investment capacity
→ future competitive capability.

Counter-pressure:

- stronger results increase salary / renewal expectations;
- sponsors may demand more;
- rivals may compete harder for talent;
- reputation/audience gains diminish at higher levels.

### Chain B — Roster investment risk

**PROPOSAL**

Roster signing
→ immediate cash / fixed commitment increase
→ competitive capability potential
→ integration / preparation demand
→ capacity pressure
→ results
→ reputation/audience upside or disappointment
→ future contract / resale / flexibility consequences.

### Chain C — Staff and delegation

**PROPOSAL**

Staff hire / authority assignment
→ cost + capacity change
→ recommendation / execution quality
→ preparation / scouting / operational outcomes
→ competitive and commercial consequences
→ trust / intervention pressure
→ future delegation design.

### Chain D — Sponsor trade-off

**PROPOSAL**

Sponsor agreement
→ scheduled cash inflow
→ obligations / restrictions
→ capacity consumption
→ audience / brand fit effect
→ relationship health
→ renewal quality / reputation effect
→ future commercial opportunity.

### Chain E — Financial pressure

**PROPOSAL**

Aggressive commitment / poor revenue
→ reduced liquidity
→ reduced flexibility
→ inability to invest / increased borrowing
→ restructuring pressure
→ roster/staff loss or worse sponsor terms
→ weaker competitive capability
→ reputation/audience pressure
→ further revenue risk.

Recovery must be possible through corrective actions before this becomes an automatic death spiral.

### Chain F — Rival competition

**PROPOSAL**

Rival strategy change
→ talent/sponsor market pressure
→ changed prices / availability
→ player forced to reprioritize
→ different roster/commercial outcome
→ altered competitive balance
→ future rival/player reputation and audience distribution.

### Chain G — Capacity overload

**PROPOSAL**

Too many commitments
→ organizational capacity overload
→ slower / lower-quality preparation, scouting, sponsor delivery, or response
→ poorer results / information
→ crises and rework
→ additional capacity pressure.

This creates a self-correcting reason not to maximize every opportunity simultaneously.

## 3. Cross-System Influence Matrix

**PROPOSAL**

| Source | Primary effects | Secondary effects |
|---|---|---|
| Competition | reputation, audience, prize income | sponsor leverage, talent attraction, expectations |
| Roster | competitive capability, payroll, flexibility | audience/star appeal, sponsor appeal, capacity load |
| Staff | delegation quality, capacity, information | development, preparation, stability |
| Finance | spending ability, distress risk | roster/staff quality, sponsor bargaining, flexibility |
| Reputation | talent/sponsor access | audience growth, negotiation leverage, expectations |
| Audience | sponsor reach/value | commercial opportunity, valuation signal |
| Sponsorship | cash, restrictions, capacity load | reputation fit, future partner access |
| Organizational Capacity | execution throughput, information quality | crisis risk, delegation effectiveness |
| Rivals | prices, opportunity availability, competitive pressure | audience share, sponsor/talent competition |

## 4. Immediate vs Delayed Consequences

### Immediate

**PROPOSAL**

Common immediate effects:

- cash paid / received;
- contract created or ended;
- roster/staff availability changed;
- roster slot occupied/freed;
- sponsor restriction activated;
- debt obligation created;
- capacity assigned;
- competition result recorded.

### Short-delay

**PROPOSAL**

Days to next match window:

- preparation quality;
- integration effects;
- staff workload outcomes;
- information updates;
- short-term form / readiness changes.

### Medium-delay

**PROPOSAL**

Several events / weeks to months:

- reputation trend;
- audience growth/decline;
- sponsor renewal quality;
- talent development;
- organizational overload consequences;
- rival strategic responses.

### Long-delay

**PROPOSAL**

Months / multiple cycles:

- debt burden consequences;
- contract inflexibility;
- company-value changes;
- sustained reputation positioning;
- rebuild success/failure;
- strategic identity emerging from repeated choices.

## 5. Feedback Requirement

**DECISION — inherited**

The player should be able to explain why the company improved or deteriorated.

**PROPOSAL**

Every major state change shown in Review should identify at least one causal category:

- player commitment;
- delegated staff action;
- competition outcome;
- rival action;
- market/world change;
- delayed effect from prior state.

## 6. Anti-Loop Requirement

**PROPOSAL**

No primary chain should be purely positive. Each growth path must create at least one of:

- higher fixed cost;
- higher expectation;
- capacity demand;
- rival reaction;
- risk exposure;
- diminishing return;
- reduced flexibility.

## 7. Open Questions

**OPEN QUESTION**

- Exact sensitivity of reputation to single-match versus sustained performance.
- Whether star players directly affect audience in the first prototype.
- How sponsor fit influences reputation without a deep brand-simulation subsystem.
